How I got here

My path into public health did not start with a spreadsheet. It started in the field, in West Africa, doing public health work that put me face to face with what happens when surveillance, data, and decisions are too slow or too disconnected from each other.

That experience is what sent me toward epidemiology rather than away from it. I came back wanting to work on the systems and the analysis behind public health decisions, not just the moment of crisis. That led me to field epidemiology at the Florida Department of Health, where I spent my days on case investigations and contact tracing during the COVID-19 response, and to early CDC surveillance work on melioidosis, a rare but serious infection where fast, accurate case identification genuinely changes outcomes.

Since 2022, I have carried that same instinct into real-world evidence and data science work at CDC's Division of Healthcare Quality Promotion: the conviction that a dataset only matters once it reaches someone who has to make a decision with it, whether that is a nursing home clinician, a CMS analyst, or a member of CDC leadership. That is the thread running through the epidemiology, RWE, biostatistics, and data science lenses on this site. They are different job titles for the same underlying habit: get the data right, then make sure it is understood.
